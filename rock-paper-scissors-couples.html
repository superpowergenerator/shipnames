How to get the most out of this game

Play it as a warm-up, not the whole evening. One best-of-5 match takes a few minutes, and it works best right before the real plan starts, like picking a film or choosing a restaurant. A quick match gets you both laughing and sets a playful tone for the night. In rock paper scissors couples, the game is the spark, not the fire, so let it light something bigger.

Agree on the prize before the first round, even if the prize is small. The winner picks the next date idea, chooses the next song, or gets out of doing the dishes tonight. A prize gives every rock paper scissors couples round a little weight, and it turns a tie into a moment instead of a shrug.

Watch your own patterns as you play, since most people repeat a favorite move. If you've thrown Rock three times in a row, the computer can't read you, yet your partner watching beside you certainly can. Noticing your own habits is part of the fun, and it makes a great talking point between rounds.

Play a second match with the roles swapped if your partner wants a turn against the computer too.
