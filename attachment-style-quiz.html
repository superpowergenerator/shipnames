Attachment Style Quiz: Find Your Style in Love

Ten quick questions about closeness, space, and comfort in love.

This attachment style quiz gives you a simple way to look at those patterns without any judgment. Answer with your first honest instinct, and you'll see which style fits you best right now.

What your result means

Your result in this attachment style quiz names the pattern you picked most often, and each style shows up in small daily moments. A secure style looks calm and steady, an anxious style watches for signs of distance, an avoidant style guards its space, and a fearful-avoidant style wants closeness and space at the same time. None of these styles makes you good or bad at love, and each one shapes how you ask for comfort, the heart of attachment theory.

Picture a partner who takes a few hours to reply to a text. A secure person waits and trusts they'll hear back soon, while an anxious person checks the phone and starts to worry they've done something wrong. An avoidant person may pull back and decide not to text again, and a fearful-avoidant person may swing between chasing a reply and shutting the chat down. Your result in this attachment style quiz points to the reaction that feels most like you.

A mix is normal too, so don't treat a close second in this attachment style quiz as a mistake in your answers. If two styles scored near the top, you've likely felt both pulls in real life, and the top one just won more of your picks today. That mix can help you spot your triggers, and it gives you a clear place to start the next talk with your partner.

What to do with your result

Once you've got your result from this attachment style quiz, share one clear example with your partner instead of handing them a label. You could say you feel calmer when you get a quick reply, or you need an hour alone after a hard day to recharge. A single example like that tells your partner what helps, and it keeps the talk about real life instead of a test score.

Pick one small change to try this week, and keep it small. If you lean anxious, you could wait ten minutes before you send a second text, then tell your partner you felt worried and ask for a quick check-in. If you lean avoidant, you could share one feeling at dinner each night, even a short one like tired or proud. Small steps like these build trust faster than a big promise you'll drop by Friday. Talk about your attachment style quiz result over dinner or on a walk, when you're both calm and you've got time to listen. Start with what you learned about yourself, then ask what your partner notices about you too, and let them answer before you jump in. If you both take the love language quiz next, you'll see how your need for closeness and your need for care fit together. That pair of results often explains more than either one on its own.

Mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake with an attachment style quiz is to treat your result as a fixed label for you or your partner. Styles can shift as trust builds, as life changes, and as you learn new ways to ask for what you need, as the Psychology Today overview of attachment explains. If you call yourself anxious in every talk, you'll start to act like that label is all you're, and your partner may hear it as an excuse instead of an honest note about how you feel.

Don't use your result to win a fight or to explain away a hurtful moment, since a quiz can't tell you who was right in a real talk. It also can't spot the full story behind a bad week, a stressful job, or a past hurt that still stings. Use this attachment style quiz to name your own needs, not to grade your partner, and keep the talk kind even when the topic feels hard.

If your result leaves you worried, talk to a trained professional for help. This quiz is a fun self-check, and it can't replace that kind of help or a real talk with someone who knows your story. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an attachment style?

+
An attachment style is a pattern in how you connect with a partner, including how you seek comfort, handle space, and respond to stress in the bond.

Is this attachment style quiz accurate?

+
It is a fun self-check, not a clinical test. It can spark useful insight, but it cannot diagnose anyone or replace a talk with a trained professional.

Can my attachment style change?

+
Yes, it can shift over time. New experiences, honest talks, and steady, kind relationships can all help you feel safer and more secure.

What if my result feels like a mix?

+
That is normal, and most people see parts of more than one style in themselves. Your top result shows your strongest pattern right now, not your whole story.
