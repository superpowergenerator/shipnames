How to get the most out of this game

Pick fast and trust your gut, since this game works best at speed. Your first instinct tells you more than a long think, and a quick pick keeps the energy high across all 40 pairs. If you pause on every pair, the game drags and the fun fades. In this or that questions for couples, a fast answer is an honest answer, so tap what jumps out at you.

Compare picks out loud after each pair, even when you match. Saying beach day before your partner says mountain hike opens a quick chat about why, and those whys are the real game. A match with different reasons can be more fun than a mismatch, since you learn you love the same thing for different causes.

Take turns tapping first so neither of you leads every round. If you always pick first, your partner starts copying you without thinking, and the game loses its surprise. Swap the lead every five pairs, and watch how your picks change when you go second.

Play it in short bursts when you've got ten free minutes. This game fits a coffee break, a bus ride, or the wait before dinner far better than a long evening, since each pair takes seconds. Ten pairs over coffee can set the tone for a whole day, and you can pick up where you left off later. Short bursts also keep the game fresh, since you never play it long enough to get bored.

What to do after the game

Look back at your matches in these this or that questions for couples and pick one to do this week. If you both chose a picnic in the park, pack one for Saturday and bring the snack you argued about. If you both chose a phone-free evening, set one for tomorrow and guard it like a date.

Use your mismatches as a trade, not a tie to break. If one of you picked coffee and the other picked tea, try one of each on your next date and rate them together. If one picked a movie night and the other picked a game night, do one this Friday and the other next Friday. Trades keep both people happy, and they turn a difference into two good nights instead of one argument.

Save your favorite pairs as prompts for future dates. A pair like sunrise walk versus sunset walk can settle a whole morning's plan, and a pair like baking cookies versus making pizza can pick your Friday night. For more pick-between fun, the would you rather game asks the same kind of question with a playful twist.

When you want a change of pace, follow this game with one that uses chance instead of choice. The couple dice game lets a roll pick your next move, which feels like a nice break after forty decisions in a row. You'll find both on the couple games page, and mixing a picking game with a rolling game keeps a long night balanced.

Mistakes to avoid

In this or that questions for couples, don't overthink a pair that has no right answer. Beach day or mountain hike isn't a life choice, and treating it like one slows the game to a crawl. If neither option thrills you, pick the one that feels closer and move on. The game only works when the stakes stay low, so keep them low on purpose.

The counter is only a count, so don't treat it like a score that matters. The counter shows how many pairs you've answered, and that's all it's for, since a high count isn't a win and a low count isn't a loss. Couples who race the counter miss the talk between pairs, which is the part worth having.

Energy dips are a sign to stop, not a reason to push through all 40 pairs. The list is long on purpose, so you can play some now and save the rest, and the game shuffles when you return. Forcing all forty in a row turns a light game into a chore, and you'll stop enjoying the picks halfway through.

Reading a pattern in your picks as proof about your partner puts them on trial. A pattern like always picking the cheaper option or the quieter night isn't evidence of anything, and reading it that way puts your partner on trial. Enjoy the pattern as a curiosity, and ask about it with a smile if it interests you. If you want slower, warmer talk after this quick game, the romantic questions for couples page gives you prompts that invite longer answers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many this or that pairs are there?

+
There are 40 original pairs. The game shuffles them and avoids repeats until you have seen them all.

How does the counter work?

+
The counter counts each pair you answer by tapping an option. Tapping Next alone does not add to the count.

Can we play this together?

+
Yes. Take turns picking, or each pick your own and compare. The fun sits in seeing where you agree.

Is this game free?

+
Yes. It is free, it runs in your browser, and you do not need to sign up or download anything.
